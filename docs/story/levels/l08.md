# Level 8 — The amount that arrives

Location ID: `workplace` · Interaction: DocumentInspector

Draft narrative; financial claims and language await sourced educator review.

## Hook

> Farah: “This is a future-adult practice payslip.”
> Farah: “Which amount can you actually plan to spend?”

## Situation

Mina looks ahead through a labelled fictional adult-income case at Farah's office. The player inspects gross pay, named deductions and take-home pay on a demo payslip. No student is asked about employment, family income or tax details.

The goal is to connect an income document to a workable budget. Farah welcomes questions about a line rather than treating confusion as carelessness.

## Trap or misconception

“The largest pay figure reaches my account,” or “every deduction is income tax.” Gross pay, deductions and net pay serve different purposes. Their meanings depend on the stated payslip and verified educational explanation.

## Consequence

A plan based on gross pay promises more spending than the simulated take-home amount supports. Identifying deductions explains the gap; using net pay gives the next decision a realistic starting point. The fictional slip is not a complete guide to anyone's real tax liability.

## Transfer twist

Provide two payslip layouts and deduction mixes. In one, net pay is not the largest or most prominent number. Locate it by meaning and reconcile the displayed breakdown, rather than tap a memorised position.

## Scripted realistic mistake path

Mina selects gross pay as the amount available to budget. Farah says, “Follow the deduction lines to the amount paid.” The player inspects each labelled line, identifies take-home pay and corrects the planned income. The changed-layout transfer is answered correctly.

Explain: a budget starts with money available after the stated deductions; unfamiliar deductions deserve a question. Legal rates and rules belong in sourced level content.
