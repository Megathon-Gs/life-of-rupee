# Level 1 — The first plan

Location ID: `home` · Interaction: BudgetAllocator

Draft narrative; financial claims and language await sourced educator review.

## Hook

> Mina: “I want the new notebook. I also need this money to last.”
> Mina: “Let me make room for both before I spend.”

## Situation

Mina receives her first fictional allowance. At home, the player allocates it among essentials, wants and a small savings share. Her study-space goal gives saving a purpose without making enjoyable purchases wrong.

The micro-lesson shows that changing one allocation changes what remains. The goal is a workable month, not the largest savings balance. No household financial details are requested.

## Trap or misconception

“If I can pay for a want today, I can afford it.” Spending the full allowance on wants overlooks essentials that arrive later. Needs and wants depend on the stated situation, not judgments about somebody's lifestyle.

## Consequence

The notebook allocation looks fine until the budget shows too little for the fictional travel expense. Mina revises it: cover essentials, retain modest savings and choose a smaller want allocation. The shortfall comes from the visible allocation, not a random punishment.

## Transfer twist

Use two stipend variants with different essentials; one introduces a surprise expense mid-month. Ask the player to revise the plan rather than repeat the original allocation or assume the same savings amount always fits.

## Scripted realistic mistake path

Mina allocates too much to the notebook and leaves travel short. Feedback says, “The notebook fits today, but the travel line is still uncovered.” The player moves money from wants to essentials, keeps feasible savings and completes the changed-stipend case.

Explain: a budget reserves money for later commitments and can change with circumstances. This ordinary corrected path targets 2 stars; a clean replay targets 3. Best stars may rise while the first attempt stays recorded. No deduction depends on reading speed.
