# Level 3 — A date for the savings

Location ID: `bank` · Interaction: TimeSlider

Draft narrative; financial claims and language await sourced educator review.

## Hook

> Leela: “A reading lamp for your desk. When will you need the money?”
> Leela: “Let's compare that date with these options.”

## Situation

Back at the bank, Mina wants to save toward a reading lamp for her study space. Leela recognises the earlier fictional account-opening lesson. The player moves a time slider to compare a savings account, recurring deposit and fixed deposit. Rates and compounding assumptions are labelled.

A larger balance is useful only alongside the goal date and access terms. Mina does not need to buy a named product.

## Trap or misconception

“The option showing the most growth is always best.” This ignores liquidity, contribution schedules and when the money is needed. Compounding frequency can change a comparison; the offered terms matter.

## Consequence

A deposit's end date falls after Mina's planned purchase date. Its attractive maturity amount is not available for the earlier goal under the stated terms. Choosing an option that fits the date makes the plan usable. Meeting the goal can acknowledge progress with the existing modest home detail; it is not a wealth score.

## Transfer twist

Provide two changed goals and timelines. One needs money sooner and makes the liquid option appropriate; the other permits a longer horizon. Change assumptions too, so the player checks access and time rather than memorises a winning card.

## Scripted realistic mistake path

Mina picks the highest maturity amount before checking its date. Leela asks, “Will it be available when you need it?” The player moves the slider to the goal date, inspects access terms and chooses the suitable option. The transfer is answered correctly.

Explain: growth, access and purpose must be compared together; real terms and rates require sourced content. This ordinary corrected path targets 2 stars. Clean replay targets 3, improving best stars without replacing the first attempt.
