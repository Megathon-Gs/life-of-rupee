## Situation

Future-adult Mina considers whether a fictional vehicle would serve a useful travel purpose. At Dev's showroom, the player compares principal, interest, fees, tenure, EMI and total repayment alongside the case budget. Postponing or declining remains legitimate.

If L5's warning-sign lesson is completed, a conditional easy-loan ad repeats its urgency cue. Dev is not its sender. Fresh profiles are not told they remember an unplayed lesson.

## Trap or misconception

“The lowest EMI makes this the cheapest or most affordable loan.” For the same principal and rate, a longer term can lower monthly payments while increasing interest under the comparison's assumptions. Interest and fees are separate parts of total borrowing cost. Repayment pressure matters; borrowing is neither inherently good nor bad.

## Consequence

The low-EMI, long-tenure preview leaves the stated budget short for essentials or planned savings and shows larger total repayment: the required clear negative borrowing outcome. Missed-payment costs follow explicit verified scenario terms, never universal legal claims.

An existing recorded installment carries into the L6 return visit's commitments. Declining borrowing creates none; vehicle ownership is not success and changes no traversal here.

## Transfer twist

Two changed offers vary tenure, fees or income. One requires postponement because neither payment fits after essentials. The eligible warning-sign callback asks the player to reject pressure and inspect terms, not accept quickly.

## Scripted realistic mistake path

Mina chooses the low EMI without checking total cost. Dev asks, “What remains each month, and for how long?” The player inspects interest, fees and the squeezed budget, then postpones in this squeezed-budget case. A higher EMI cannot solve the same shortfall. The new-offer transfer is answered correctly.

Explain: monthly affordability and total cost are separate checks. This ordinary corrected path targets 2 stars; clean replay targets 3, preserving the first result. Paying fraud is critical: unresolved earns 0; corrected caps that attempt at 1.
