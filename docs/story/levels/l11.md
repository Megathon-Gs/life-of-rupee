# Level 11 — A goal before a return

Location ID: `learning-centre` · Interaction: OfferComparator, then TimeSlider

Draft narrative; financial claims and language await sourced educator review.

## Hook

> Suri: “Before we compare returns, when do you need the money?”
> Suri: “Let's try more than one possible outcome.”

## Situation

At the learning centre, Mina compares generic FD, bond, mutual-fund and equity cards for a fictional goal. Each shows relevant risk, liquidity and time horizon. The slider displays illustrative ranges labelled “Assumption,” including unfavorable outcomes where relevant.

This builds understanding, not a recommendation to invest student money. It names no real fund, scheme, company or product. Earlier saving and inflation lessons give goals and future prices context.

## Trap or misconception

“The highest illustrated return is guaranteed and therefore best.” A favourable example is not a promise. Access, risk and the goal date matter; spreading exposure can reduce concentration without eliminating all risk.

## Consequence

An optimistic outcome looks sufficient, but another illustration leaves the goal short when money is needed. Comparing ranges and access terms shows why a near-term essential goal needs different reasoning from a long-term flexible one. No instrument is a universal winner.

## Transfer twist

Two goals change horizon and ability to absorb loss. A near-term essential expense calls for a suitable lower-risk, accessible option in the stated case. A longer horizon requires comparing trade-offs rather than automatically selecting equities.

## Scripted realistic mistake path

Mina chooses the highest favourable projection. Suri asks, “What if you need it during the lower outcome?” The player moves the slider, examines the range and access conditions, then matches an option to the goal. The new-horizon transfer is answered correctly.

Explain: compare risk, liquidity, diversification and time together; simulated returns are assumptions, not promises. This ordinary corrected path targets 2 stars.
